Before your first visit

  1. 1

    Book your appointment

    Choose a physiotherapy initial consultation, or an exercise physiology initial assessment if you already know what the problem is.

  2. 2

    Fill in the intake form

    We'll email a short form when you book. It takes about five minutes and means more of your session is spent on you.

  3. 3

    Gather anything relevant

    Scan reports, surgery notes and letters from your doctor or surgeon all help. Bring the shoes you walk or train in most.

  4. 4

    Pay just the gap

    We claim your health fund rebate on the spot through HICAPS, so you only pay the difference on the day.

How your sessions work

Your first session runs for [45–60] minutes. We'll talk through what's happening, test strength and movement, explain what we find, and agree on a plan, including roughly how many sessions you're likely to need and what that will cost.

Follow-up sessions focus on treatment and progressing your exercises. We re-test at key points so you can see your progress, and we'll bring in our exercise physiologist when strength work becomes the main job.

Common questions

Do I need a referral from my GP?
No. Physiotherapists and exercise physiologists are first-contact practitioners, so you can book with us directly. If you do have a referral or scans, bring them along.
How much will my health fund pay?
It depends on your fund and level of extras cover. Call your fund with the item numbers below to check your rebate and how much of your yearly limit is left: physiotherapy initial [500], follow-up [505]; exercise physiology [CONFIRM item numbers with your billing software].
Is exercise physiology covered by my health fund?
Many extras policies cover exercise physiology, sometimes under a separate limit from physio. Check with your fund before booking.
What should I wear?
Comfortable clothes you can move in. Shorts are ideal for hip, knee and ankle assessments. Bring your usual walking, running or training shoes.
Do I need a scan first?
Usually not. Most hip, knee and ankle problems can be assessed without imaging. If a scan would change your plan, we'll explain why and work with your GP to arrange it.
