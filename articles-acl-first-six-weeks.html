The first six weeks after ACL reconstruction: what to focus on

The early weeks set the foundation for everything that follows. Here's what matters most, what's normal, and when to call your surgeon.

MW Matt WisemanPhysiotherapist and Accredited Exercise Physiologist · [Month Year]

Key points

  • Early priorities: settle swelling, get the knee fully straight, switch the quads back on and walk normally.
  • Your surgeon's protocol comes first, especially if you also had a meniscus repair.
  • Progress by milestones, not just the calendar.
  • Returning to sport usually takes at least 9 months.

Weeks 0–2: settle it down and switch it on

The first fortnight is about calming the knee and restoring the basics. Four things matter most:

  • Swelling. Ice or a cold-compression device, a compression sleeve and elevation. A swollen knee switches off the quadriceps, so controlling swelling makes everything else easier.
  • Getting fully straight. Full extension, matching your other knee, is the top early goal. Rest with your heel propped on a towel, and avoid sleeping with a pillow under the knee.
  • Quads on. Thigh squeezes and straight leg raises, once you can lift without the knee bending. If the muscle won't fire, we may use electrical stimulation to help.
  • Walking. Crutches at first, with weight-bearing as your surgeon advises. Gradual bending with heel slides, aiming for around 90 degrees by about two weeks unless your surgeon says otherwise.

Weeks 2–6: build control

As swelling settles, the focus shifts to strength and normal movement.

  • Off the crutches once you have full extension, good quad control and can walk without a limp.
  • Strength work such as mini squats, leg press in a safe range, bridges, calf raises and hip exercises. Your graft type changes some choices; hamstring grafts need a gentler start on hamstring loading.
  • A stationary bike once the knee bends far enough to pedal comfortably.
  • Balance starting with single-leg standing.
  • Train the other leg too. It keeps you strong overall and gives us a benchmark to compare against later.

Milestones to aim for by around six weeks

  • Knee fully straight, matching the other side
  • Bend of around 120 degrees, or as your surgeon advises
  • Little or no swelling after activity
  • Straight leg raise with no bend at the knee
  • Walking without crutches or a limp

What's normal, and what isn't

Swelling, bruising that tracks down the shin, morning stiffness and a numb patch beside the scar are all common in the early weeks.

Contact your surgeon or GP promptly if you notice:

  • Calf pain, swelling or redness (possible blood clot)
  • Fever, or a wound that is red, hot or oozing
  • A sudden jump in pain or swelling
  • The knee locking or suddenly losing movement

Chest pain or sudden shortness of breath: call 000.

The long game

Clinical guidelines recommend progressing ACL rehab by meeting criteria rather than by time alone.2,3 From around three months, strength and hop testing help decide when to start running, jumping and changing direction.

Returning to sport too early is a real risk. In one well-known study, people who returned to pivoting sport before nine months, or before passing strength and function tests, had a much higher rate of reinjury.1 We test along the way so you know exactly where you stand.
